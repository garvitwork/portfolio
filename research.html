The Negation Circuit in GPT-2 Small

GPT-2 flips its sentiment prediction under negation — "the film was really good" becomes "did not think the film was really bad". Which internal components cause that flip, and is it one head or a distributed circuit?

Question

Which attention heads and MLP layers causally mediate negation's effect on sentiment prediction — and does the effect localize to one component, or spread across several?

Method

  1. Minimal-pair dataset — 960 templated prompts, each ending right before a single-token adjective, so the model's prediction can be read as one clean number: logit_diff = logit(positive_adj) − logit(negative_adj).
  2. Activation patching — patch each of the 144 attention heads and 12 MLP layers from a clean (affirmative) run into a corrupted (negated) run, and measure how much of the effect each one recovers.
  3. Direct logit attribution — an independent, non-patching method: decompose the final prediction additively across every head via the residual stream, to cross-check the first method.
  4. Ablation — zero out the implicated heads and confirm the effect actually breaks, moving from correlation to causation.

Findings

L10H9 is the strongest signal in both independent methods — highest recovery under patching, and the single largest-magnitude score under direct logit attribution. But ablating it alone barely moves the behavior. Ablating the top 10 implicated heads together removes 37% of the effect. The circuit is distributed and redundant, not localized to one head — the same "backup head" pattern documented in GPT-2's IOI circuit (Wang et al., 2022), here reproduced independently for a different behavior.
